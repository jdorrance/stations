XCTrack widget

Nearest wind stations, one tile each. Position comes from XCTrack.

  1. In the layout editor, add a Web page widget (XCTrack Pro).
  2. Paste the URL. Turn on Allow web page to access XCTrack data. Set reload to 0 — the page polls itself.
  3. Place the widget. It needs a network connection.

URL

Size 0 is the small tile. 2–100 enlarges it. Leave categories unchecked for all of them.

Correct the highlighted settings before copying or opening the widget.

Fixed position

A fixed position overrides XCTrack and browser GPS. lat and lng go together.

Parameters

unitsmetric km/h and km, the default. imperial mph and miles. kts knots, distances in km. ms m/s, distances in km. Also accepted: mph, kmh, kt, knots, mps, m/s.
size2–100. Tiles scale by 1 + size/100. 0 or 1 leaves them at 80 px.
modedark.
lat, lngFixed position. Both are required. lon works in place of lng.
altYour altitude in metres. The tick on each station’s height rail. The slider appears once this is set and a station on screen has a height; moving it overrides the fix for that session. The title still calls a site a peak when it is more than 80 m above you.
maxageHide readings older than this many minutes. Enter a whole number of at least 1. Default 120.
categoryComma list of freeflight, mountain, synoptic, marine, pws. Default is all of them.
debugAny value. Pretend the fix is Mt Hatis and drift it. For testing, not for flying.

Reading a tile

The rose is the wind: average over gust, arrow flies with the wind, top tick is north. Colour follows the average: blue under 5 km/h, green 5–9, gold 10–14, orange 15–24, deep orange 25–39. Red, and a tinted tile, is 40 km/h or more — past trim. A gust in a higher band steps the colour up one. A faint dashed circle outside the rose is where the station is; the solid arc on it is the direction, and the footer is the station metres on the left and ~ and the distance on the right. With no station height, the distance stays centered. A rail on the left is height among the stations on this screen: the bead is this station, the tick is your altitude, and the bar between them is the gap. With no altitude yet, the metres are the station’s own. The slider along the bottom is shown once a fix or the alt parameter has your altitude and a station on screen has a height. Moving it overrides that altitude for the session. Past the highest or lowest station, the tick becomes an arrow. No age chip means the reading is live. Then amber, dark brown, and black — three fixed pairs, so the digits stay readable — while the tile fades. A stale arrow is an outline on a dashed ring. A station with no reading is left off the screen. A green arc on the wind ring is that station’s launch direction, when it declares one.
